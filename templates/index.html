Your credentials are too sensitive for a password manager, too fragile for paper. A hardware wallet doesn't help with your 2FA backup codes or your root passwords. So where does it go?

What it's for

Your protocol identity

Nostr keys, Bluesky keys. Lose the key, lose the identity. No recovery form, no customer support — just your stewards.

Your homelab

Server recovery keys, 2FA backup codes, root passwords. The thing only you have, that unlocks everything else.

Your digital will

Estate planning, framed as the same engineer at a different moment. Credentials your family would need if you're gone.

{# ── How it works ─────────────────────────────────────────────── #}

How it works

01

Split

Your secret is split into encrypted keys. No single key reveals anything.

02

Distribute

Each key goes to a steward you choose, peer-to-peer. No server sees it.

03

Recover

A threshold of your stewards approve, and the vault reassembles on your device.

{# ── What it is / isn't ──────────────────────────────────────── #}

What it is, what it isn't

It is

  • The social layer of your security stack.
  • For credentials you're comfortable putting on a phone.
  • Open source, MIT, funded by OpenSats.

It isn't

  • A password manager — it's for what's behind it.
  • A hardware wallet — backup, not signing.
  • A cloud service — no servers own your data.
  • A replacement for serious crypto custody — yet.
