About

The idea for Horcrux was inspired by a password-sharing app called Dark Crystal, which let you split a secret into pieces and distribute them among friends. Matt found himself dreaming of a tool where he could share a vault with close friends — a place to store important credentials, keys, and digital-will information — and set a threshold so that only a subset of those friends together could open it.

That dream led to a simple question: what if you could split your most sensitive data into encrypted shares and give one to each person you trust? No single share would be enough to decrypt the vault, but when enough stewards agreed to help, the vault would unlock.

Horcrux is built as a convivial tool — software that serves people directly rather than extracting value from them. It is fully open source, free to use and modify, and part of the digital commons.

You choose your own stewards, set your own recovery thresholds, and run your own relays if you wish. The project is designed to outlast any single contributor or organization — because your data shouldn't depend on a company's bottom line to stay safe.

Get involved

Horcrux is early and there is lots to do. Questions, feedback, and contributions are all welcome.
