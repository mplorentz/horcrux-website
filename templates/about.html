About

Horcrux was created by Matt Lorentz in 2025 with support from an OpenSats grant. It is free and open source software, MIT licensed.

The idea for Horcrux was inspired by a scene in the 1982 film The Dark Crystal, in which the Skeksis pour their essence into a vessel outside of themselves. Today our secrets — keys, passwords, recovery phrases — live in the cloud or nowhere at all. We wanted a different way: store your most sensitive data encrypted and sharded across devices held by people you trust, so losing one device doesn't mean losing the secret, and no single steward can act alone.

Horcrux is a convivial tool — it exists to empower its users rather than to extract from them. There is no server-side master key, no subscription, no data-mining, no growth target. The app connects peers directly over the Nostr protocol using open relays that anyone can run. The cryptography (secp256k1, ChaCha20, Shamir's Secret Sharing) is standard, well-studied, and implemented in the open. Horcrux is designed to be durable: the underlying technology is stable and boring on purpose, so the app keeps working long after any one company would have shut it down.
